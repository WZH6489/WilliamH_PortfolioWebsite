Respite

A native iOS digital wellness app — built entirely in Swift with a team of four mentor-guided developers — that uses on-device signals to nudge people toward calmer, more deliberate phone use.

  • Swift
  • Xcode
  • Liquid Glass
  • Screen Time
  • HealthKit
  • WeatherKit
  • Live Activities

The cohort

Respite is the four-month build I’m shipping inside the StormForge 2026 Cohort at SFU Surge — mentor-guided product work selected from a competitive applicant pool. Our team of four owns design, engineering, and delivery end-to-end, with weekly reviews from industry mentors.

What it does

Respite is a context-aware digital wellness companion. Rather than blocking apps with a wall of red, it reads in-the-moment signals — your screen time today, recent activity, even the weather outside — and suggests small acts of respite: a walk, a stretch, a breath, a few minutes off-grid.

The intent is to replace willpower-driven app blockers with something gentler and more honest about how people actually relate to their phones.

What I’ve built

  • Core iOS application logic in Swift — view models, state management, and the rules engine that turns sensor inputs into actionable suggestions.
  • Liquid Glass UI components built in Xcode — translucent cards, depth-aware blurs, and motion that respects prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Screen Time integration to surface app usage without leaving the sandbox.
  • HealthKit for active-energy and step context — used to time nudges around real movement.
  • WeatherKit to bias outdoor suggestions against current conditions.
  • Live Activities for glanceable session feedback on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island.

What I’m learning

  • Shipping inside Apple’s permissions model — how to ask once, well.
  • Designing UI that looks composed at 60 fps and stays readable on a Lock Screen widget.
  • Working in a small team with mentors — feedback loops, scope discipline, when to say no.

Status

In active development. Cohort demos in April 2026 — case study will be expanded with screens, metrics, and lessons once the build ships.
